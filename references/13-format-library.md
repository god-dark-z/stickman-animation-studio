# 13 — Format library, hook bank and story arcs

The six-beat structure in `04-script-craft.md` is **one** format, not the format. Two videos on
different topics should not feel like clones. Pick a format from this library by topic, then use
its beats — the six-beat "paradox" shape is the default when nothing else fits.

Every format below is written so it works in English **and** Bangla: the beats are about what the
viewer learns and feels, not about English idiom. Word budgets come from the language pack
(60 s ≈ 200 English words / 165 Bangla words).

## How to choose

| If the topic is… | Use | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| a surprising mechanism ("why does X happen") | **Explainer with mystery** | the gap between "what you see" and "why" *is* the hook |
| a real person's life or death | **True story** | names and dates carry weight; the arc is already there |
| a documented event with a date | **Historical event** | stakes are free; the ending is known and still lands |
| something people repeat wrongly | **Myth vs fact** | the correction is the payoff |
| machinery, biology, a process | **How it works** | natural three-step spine, perfect for inserts |
| something absurd in ordinary life | **Comedy skit** | the mock line is the engine |
| a lesson the viewer needs | **Motivational parable** | short, universal, ends on a turn |
| a task the viewer will do | **Tutorial** | step → do → result; inserts are the payload |
| several facts in one topic | **News-style recap** | fast cuts, one fact per beat, no fake arc |
| two people in conflict | **Character-driven mini drama** | dialogue + reaction beats |

Never pick a format that needs facts you do not have. A thin **true story** is worse than a solid
**explainer**.

## 1. Explainer with mystery (the default)

**Beats (6):** paradox hook → setup → mock line → turning point → "you see" mechanism + insert →
twist.

- **Hook:** the observable fact, stated plainly, no question mark needed.
  `Your phone gets hotter the worse your signal is.`
- **Setup:** who or what is affected, one sentence, one image.
- **Mock:** someone dismisses it ("that's just how phones work").
- **Turn:** the question that reframes it: "so why does *searching* make heat?"
- **Mechanism:** one analogy from ordinary life + one insert (chart/counter/diagram).
- **Twist:** the consequence the viewer did not expect ("the heat is the phone protecting itself").

**Shot grammar:** hook = wide establishing; setup = medium; mock = two-shot or close on the
scoffer; mechanism = insert + hands/objects; twist = close on the object or a reveal.

**Ending options:** protective irony, the cost you did not see, the thing you can now do.

**Worked example (EN, ~60 s, 198 words):**
> 1. Your phone gets hotter the worse your signal is. *(hook, wide street)*
> 2. In a basement, in a lift, on a train through a tunnel — the bars drop and the phone warms up. *(setup)*
> 3. "It's just a phone," my friend said. "They all do that." *(mock, close on friend)*
> 4. But yours does not warm up when the signal is strong. So what is it doing differently? *(turn)*
> 5. You see, a weak signal is a faint voice across a noisy room. The phone turns its own volume up — more transmit power, more amplifier current, more heat. *(mechanism + insert: power bars)*
> 6. It is not broken. It is shouting. *(twist)*

**Worked example (BN, ~60 s, 168 words):** same beats, চলিত register, one local analogy
(রিকশাওয়ালা খালি রাস্তায় আরও জোরে ডাকেন), insert labels in Bangla digits.

## 2. True story

**Beats (6):** the outcome stated cold (hook) → the person in their ordinary life → the first
fracture → the decision → the cost → what it left behind.

Rules: one person, one decision, one cost. Dates only where they matter. Never invent dialogue for
a real person — quote only what is documented, and mark a paraphrase as paraphrase. The fact-check
table is mandatory and every date gets a source.

## 3. Historical event

**Beats (6):** the strange detail (hook) → the world before → the trigger → the event itself →
the human cost → the thing that is still true.

Rules: the ending is known, so **do not** build suspense about the outcome — build it about the
*price*. Numbers must be sourced; if two sources disagree, say both.

## 4. Myth vs fact

**Beats (5):** the myth as most people say it → why it feels true → the evidence that breaks it →
the accurate version → why the myth survives.

Rules: attack the claim, never the person who believes it. The "why it survives" beat is what
separates this from a lecture.

## 5. How it works

**Beats (5):** the end result (hook) → the three stages, one shot each → the failure mode when a
stage breaks → the everyday analogy → the "so that's why" close.

Rules: three stages, never five. Each stage gets an insert. The analogy must be physical
(something the viewer has touched).

## 6. Comedy skit

**Beats (5):** a mundane situation → an absurd escalation → the mock line → the even more absurd
response → deadpan button.

Rules: two characters maximum, one location, no narration over the joke. The silence after the
button line is the joke — hold the shot 0.6 s longer.

## 7. Motivational parable

**Beats (5):** a small ordinary task → the obstacle → the choice → the visible consequence → a
single line of meaning (never a speech).

Rules: no "you can do it". The meaning line must be concrete. One character, one place.

## 8. Tutorial

**Beats (5):** the result the viewer wants (hook, show it) → the prerequisite → step 1, step 2,
step 3 (one shot each) → the common mistake → the finished result again.

Rules: every step is an insert or a clear hand/object shot. No step may be spoken without being
shown — the sync gate will catch it.

## 9. News-style recap

**Beats (4–6):** the headline fact → context in one sentence → two or three developments, one per
shot → what happens next (stated as unknown if it is unknown).

Rules: fastest cutting of any format (1.5–2.5 s shots), no mock line, no twist. Facts only, each
with a source.

## 10. Character-driven mini drama

**Beats (6):** the two characters in their normal relation → the small ask → the refusal →
the consequence → the reversal → the new relation.

Rules: two characters, distinct voices (`choose_voice.ts` enforces gender/rate distinctness), real
dialogue with short lines (≤ 12 words so mouths read), reactions on every line. This is the format
that uses character voices.

## Hook bank (line 1, stated as fact — never a greeting)

| Pattern | English | Bangla |
|---|---|---|
| Observable oddity | "Your phone gets hotter the worse your signal is." | "সিগন্যাল যত দুর্বল, ফোন তত গরম।" |
| Number that should not be | "Twenty thousand soldiers lost to birds." | "বিশ হাজার সৈন্য হেরে গেল পাখির কাছে।" |
| Reversal of expectation | "The machine that saved the factory was the one that broke it." | "কারখানা বাঁচানো মেশিনটাই সেটা ভেঙেছিল।" |
| Cost you did not see | "Every 'free' app is paid for by something you cannot see." | "প্রতিটা 'ফ্রি' অ্যাপের দাম দেয় অন্য কিছু।" |
| Ordinary thing, strange name | "There is a name for the sound your fridge makes at night." | "রাতে ফ্রিজের শব্দটারও একটা নাম আছে।" |
| Someone was wrong, loudly | "In 1995 an expert said the internet was a fad." | "১৯৯৫ সালে এক বিশেষজ্ঞ বলেছিলেন ইন্টারনেট ফ্যাশন।" |

Banned first lines: "Did you know", "In this video", "Today we will", "Let's talk about", "Have you
ever wondered", "আজ আমরা জানব", "আপনি কি জানেন".

## Title and first three seconds

- **Title:** 4–7 words, concrete noun, no clickbait colon chains. It should be the hook compressed.
- **Seconds 0–3:** the hook line spoken over the establishing shot; the subject must be on screen
  before the first word ends. No logo, no intro card, no music-only open.
- **Seconds 3–6:** a second image that raises the question — the place, the person, the object that
  will matter.

## Emotional arc

Every format moves the viewer along one axis:

| Arc | Start | Middle | End | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Curiosity → understanding | "why?" | "wait, really?" | "oh — of course" | explainer, how-it-works |
| Unease → dread → relief | "something is off" | "it is worse than I thought" | "it was survivable" | true story, historical |
| Confidence → doubt → clarity | "obviously X" | "…or is it?" | "X, but for a reason" | myth vs fact |
| Warmth → loss → warmth | "ordinary happiness" | "the thing is taken" | "what remains" | parable, drama |

Write the arc down in `script.md` before the shots. If the arc is flat, the video will be too.

## Repetition guard (two videos must not feel like clones)

Before storyboarding, compare with your last video on these five axes and change **at least three**:

1. **Hook pattern** (from the bank above).
2. **Format** (from the library).
3. **Opening framing** (wide establishing vs close detail vs two-shot).
4. **Insert type** (chart vs counter vs versus vs bigtext).
5. **Ending move** (irony vs number vs image vs line of dialogue).

The sync gate already flags three identical framing+camera shots in a row; this guard works one
level up, across videos.

## Beat-to-shot budget

| Video length | Shots | Avg shot | Inserts | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30–40 s | 7–10 | 3.5 s | 1 | recap, myth vs fact |
| 45–60 s | 10–16 | 3.5–4 s | 1–2 | explainer, how-it-works, true story |
| 60–75 s | 14–20 | 3.5–4 s | 2 | historical, drama |
| 75–90 s | 18–26 | 3.2 s | 2–3 | tutorial, long explainer |

Longer videos do not get longer shots — they get **more** shots. A 6 s shot is a dead shot unless
it is a deliberate hold (a silent reaction, a reveal).
